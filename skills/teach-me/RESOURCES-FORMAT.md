# Course Sources and References

Apply this guidance when researching the course and authoring `reference/resources.html`. Sources support the explanations; they are not a substitute for writing them.

## Source selection and verification

- Prefer official documentation, specifications and standards, original papers, authoritative books, and engineering write-ups with explicit mechanisms and evidence. Use secondary explanations when they genuinely clarify a concept.
- Verify that a source exists and supports the linked claim when web or repository access is available. Check relevant versions and distinguish guarantees from implementation details.
- Mark an inaccessible or unverified resource explicitly. If access is unavailable, state that limitation and distinguish sourced facts from uncertain claims. Do not invent URLs, citations, quotations, or verification results.
- Choose sources relevant to the scoped course, rather than filling a reading list with loosely related links.

## Citations within chapters

Place descriptive links beside the explanations they support, especially guarantees, algorithms, performance claims, and version-specific behavior. Link to the relevant section rather than a site's homepage when possible. Explain the mechanism in the chapter so following the link is optional.

Distinguish a measured result from a general rule, and identify assumptions behind performance comparisons. Label illustrative examples as such instead of attributing them to a source that does not contain them.

## References page

Create `reference/resources.html` with the shared course navigation. Group entries by chapter or technical theme. Each entry contains:

- A descriptive linked title and author or publisher.
- A short annotation explaining its coverage and when deeper reading is useful.
- The version, publication date, or edition when technically relevant.
- Links back to the chapter sections it supports.
- A verification/access note when the resource could not be checked.

Include a source-limitations section when consequential gaps remain. External references may require internet access; all course explanations and diagrams remain locally readable.
